Safer communities

Use the mayoralty to support confident neighbourhoods, safer streets and stronger local partnerships.

People should feel confident in the places they live.

Safety is not only about headline crime figures. It is also about whether people feel comfortable walking through their town centre, whether antisocial behaviour is dealt with and whether neighbourhood problems are allowed to persist.

The mayor should use regional leadership to help local services work together earlier, share what works and focus on the problems residents are actually reporting.

01

Focus on neighbourhood confidence

Visible problems matter. Persistent antisocial behaviour, vandalism and disorder can change how people feel about an area even when they never become major crimes. Local action should respond quickly and visibly.

02

Support practical action on antisocial behaviour

The mayor can help bring police, councils, transport providers and community organisations around shared plans for problem locations rather than allowing responsibility to bounce between agencies.

03

Put prevention alongside enforcement

Enforcement matters, but so does stopping problems becoming entrenched. The region should make better use of data, youth services, environmental design and local partnerships to intervene earlier.
